About

I am a master's student in Social Medicine and Health Management at the School of Public Health (Shenzhen), now the School of Biosafety, Sun Yat-sen University. Before joining Sun Yat-sen University, I received my bachelor's degree in Agricultural Economy and Management from Nanjing Agricultural University. My research focuses on artificial intelligence in healthcare, big data medical research, digital health, and healthy aging.

I have conducted several research such as using large cohort or EHR data for predictive modeling and personalized interventions, with the goal of improving patient outcomes or promoting healthy aging. I am familiar with the techniques including epidemiological models, machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and large language models (LLMs).

One potentially interesting point is my interdisciplinary background, which gives me a broader academic perspective and versatile skills. My undergraduate training involved intensive coursework in mathematics, statistics, and programming, equipping me with strong logical thinking and modeling abilities. My master's program laid a solid foundation in medical research, while I have been actively exploring the intersection of AI and healthcare. In the future, I hope to become a data scientist in the healthcare field, and I have been working steadily toward this goal.

Phenotype identification and precise intervention for multimorbidity of rheumatoid arthritis and diabetes mellitus using interpretable machine learning

Zhijun He, Jinglin Han, Xinzhu Qiao, Yiqiang Zhan, Mingming Xu

Digital Health

This paper builds an interpretable machine learning framework to classify RA-DM multimorbidity and uncover phenotype heterogeneity. It combines optimized XGBoost, SHAP interpretation, and SOM/K-means clustering to translate model outputs into phenotype-specific intervention insights.

Uncovering multimorbidity patterns linked to disability in aging populations: A machine learning analysis

Zhijun He, Xingtong Pei, Xiaofeng Li, Yang Zhao, Mingming Xu

Geriatrics & Gerontology International

This study identifies clinically meaningful multimorbidity patterns among older adults and quantifies their associations with disability. It shows how machine learning and regression modeling can support tailored multimorbidity management for aging populations.

The impact of dynamic income disparities on disability progression in older adults: A joint group-based trajectory modeling study

Zhijun He, Jinglin Han, Xinyi Huang, Mingming Xu

Journal of Applied Gerontology

This article models joint income and disability trajectories to understand how socioeconomic dynamics shape functional aging. It highlights age-specific differences and points to targeted financial support and preventive healthcare as potential strategies.
